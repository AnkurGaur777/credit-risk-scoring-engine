# Model card: Credit risk scoring (PD model)

> **Read this first:** every number below comes from the **synthetic stand-in dataset** generated by `credit_risk.synthetic` (same columns and quirks as Home Credit, but fake people). It proves the pipeline works end to end; it says nothing about real borrowers. Re-run on the real Kaggle data (`CR_DATA_SOURCE=real`) and regenerate these documents with `make docs` before quoting any figure.

**Version:** 0.1.0 | **Date:** 2026-11-17 | **Status:** educational demonstration

## 1. Purpose and intended use
Estimate the **probability of default (PD)** for a loan application at decision time, convert it to a score,
give plain-English reasons, and support an approve/decline cut-off chosen on expected profit.
Intended for portfolio/learning use and as a template for how a risk team documents a model.

## 2. Out-of-scope use
Not a production lending system and not financial advice. Not validated for any real lender, product, country or period;
no legal, RBI/digital-lending or fair-lending compliance review has been performed. Must not be used for individual
credit decisions. Not suitable for pricing, limit setting or loss provisioning without LGD/EAD models.

## 3. Data
* Source: Home Credit Default Risk (Kaggle competition data; license/rules apply, raw data is not redistributed here).
* This build: **synthetic** data, 20,000 applications,
  default rate 8.17% (train).
* Target: client had payment difficulties (TARGET = 1).
* Split: stratified 60/20/20 with fixed seed 42; test set used once for final numbers.
* No application date exists, so no out-of-time validation (limitation).

## 4. Preprocessing
Sentinel `DAYS_EMPLOYED = 365243` -> NaN + flag; `XNA` -> NaN; negative day counts converted to years; ratios
(payment burden, loan/income, loan/goods); missing-score flags; rare categories grouped on train only.
Features used: 53. Excluded by policy: AGE_YEARS, CODE_GENDER, DAYS_BIRTH, EMPLOYED_TO_AGE_RATIO.

## 5. Models compared
| Model | Role |
|---|---|
| WoE scorecard (monotone binning, logistic regression; IV 0.02-0.5 and |corr| < 0.7; 12 final variables) | Interpretable, regulator-friendly baseline |
| LightGBM (imbalance strategy: class_weight; 12 Optuna trials; early stopping) | Challenger, then calibrated (platt) |
| XGBoost | Comparison only |

Scorecard scaling: base score 600 at good:bad odds 50:1, 20 points to double the odds.

## 6. Performance (test set, 95% bootstrap CI)
| | AUC | Gini | KS | PR-AUC | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scorecard | 0.683 [0.657, 0.717] | 0.365 | 0.286 [0.250, 0.352] | 0.168 | 0.0729 |
| LightGBM (calibrated) | 0.693 [0.671, 0.727] | 0.386 | 0.313 [0.274, 0.378] | 0.180 | 0.0718 |
| Stage | Result |
|---|---|
| Stage B (all tables) | LightGBM test AUC 0.745, KS 0.378; scorecard AUC 0.735 |

A gap of a few thousandths of AUC between scorecard and GBM is not evidence that the GBM is better;
see the confidence intervals. Anything near AUC 0.9 on this kind of data should be investigated for leakage.

## 7. Calibration
Calibrator chosen by 2-fold cross-fitting on the validation set, fit on validation, evaluated on test.

| model | method | brier | ece | mean_pred |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LightGBM | raw | 0.1750 | 0.2848 | 0.3666 |
| LightGBM | platt | 0.0718 | 0.0089 | 0.0813 |
| LightGBM | isotonic | 0.0724 | 0.0101 | 0.0811 |
| Scorecard | scorecard (logistic, raw) | 0.0729 | 0.0139 | 0.0820 |

## 8. Explainability
TreeSHAP on LightGBM (global summary + dependence plots in `reports/figures/`). Per-applicant **reason codes** = the top
features pushing risk up, mapped to customer-friendly sentences (`explain.REASON_CODES`) and de-duplicated.
Rank agreement between mean |SHAP| and scorecard IV: Spearman 0.84.
SHAP explains the model, not causation.

## 9. Fairness
Audited by gender, age band and family status at the chosen cut-off (gender is not a model input; proxies can still leak).
* Gender: approval-rate gap 0.002, disparate-impact ratio 0.998, four-fifths flag False.
* Age band: approval-rate gap 0.040, disparate-impact ratio 0.953, four-fifths flag False.
Group-specific thresholds are legally sensitive and are not applied. Remaining gaps may reflect real risk differences,
proxies, or sample noise in small groups; they should be reviewed by compliance before any real use.

## 10. Business cut-off (illustrative economics)
| assumption | value |
|---|---|
| margin_rate | 0.10 |
| lgd | 0.70 |
| cost_per_application | 0.00 |

Cut-off chosen on validation: approve if PD <= 0.136. Test: approval 83.8%,
bad rate among approved 6.18%. Profit vs approve-all is a **simulation** driven by these
assumptions; see the +/-20% sensitivity table in `reports/results_*.md`.

## 11. Limitations
Single lender and country; old data; no macro-economic information; labels only exist for **approved** applicants
(selection bias, no reject inference); no application date so no out-of-time test; calibration is only as good as the
development population; synthetic numbers (if present) are not evidence of real performance; LGD and margin are assumed.

## 12. Monitoring
See `docs/monitoring_plan.md`: score/feature PSI, approval rate, bad rate by cohort, calibration, AUC/KS on matured cohorts,
fairness, data quality. PSI thresholds 0.10 / 0.25 are conventions.

## 13. Ethical considerations
Credit decisions affect people's lives. Use human review for borderline cases, give adverse-action reasons, keep audit
logs without personal data, and re-audit fairness at each retrain. Check current RBI digital-lending and fair-practice
guidance before any real deployment.
